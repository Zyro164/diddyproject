Free Fire guild glory bots

What the services actually do, what the arithmetic says, and what happens to the guild afterwards.

“Guild glory bot” names three unrelated things that are constantly conflated: a real in-game metric with hard daily caps, a cottage industry of cloud-phone farms and resold APIs, and a YouTube-to-Telegram funnel that mostly sells attention. The useful move is separating them — and that is possible with arithmetic, because the metric is capped. Garena’s own rules limit a player to roughly 80 glory a day from matches. Four bots therefore top out near 320 a day. Vendors advertise 2,00,000 glory per ₹100 credit.

Verdict: do not buy. Not primarily on ethics grounds — on unit economics and enforcement risk.

• The advertised yield is 120–330× the per-hour maximum the game allows. Either the number is fiction, or delivery happens server-side (packet forgery / API injection), which is the one thing Garena bans devices for.

• Enforcement went guild-level in 2026: Garena’s Abuse Policy authorises removal of guild points, tournament disqualification and permanent disbandment for “coordinated use of prohibited tools”.

• At its honest yield, ₹100 buys about 3% of a single early level-up — roughly what four recruited players produce for free in a day.

1. The product on sale

Every offer in this market resolves to the same shape: pay ₹95–₹200, submit a Guild ID (not a login), and a group of accounts plays matches inside your guild for 8–12 hours. The differences between vendors are marketing, region, and whether they also sell cheat licences.

The adjacent products matter for judging the market: the same panels sell profile likes and mod-menu keys alongside “guild glory”. A vendor whose other SKU is an aimbot licence cannot credibly underwrite a “100% anti-ban guarantee” — the anti-ban claim is the product, not the infrastructure.

2. What glory actually is — the caps nobody quotes

Garena runs two overlapping systems and its own documentation has not kept up with the client. Glory is the legacy level currency described in the still-published help articles. Activity Points replaced it as the level driver in the OB42 “Guild System 2.0” patch (Oct 2023), with dynamic weekly settlement and decay. Community trackers now show guilds up to Lv.7 with ~50–55 slots, while Garena’s own 2024 help page still says “level 4, 35 members”. Vendors quote whichever number is larger.

The third row is the one that guts the business model. Glory is not a currency you can inject — it is a rate-limited telemetry output. Four bots are four players. They earn what four players earn, no matter how the vendor describes the “engine”.

What ₹100 actually buys

  • ~250–320 glory at the ceiling — about 3% of the 10,000 needed for Lv.2.
  • One day of four ghost members in a roster that likely has 20–55 slots.
  • Compared with the diamond route, ~17,000× cheaper — which is exactly why the 2-lakh number cannot be real. At Garena’s own price, 2,00,000 glory costs ₹16.8 lakh (10💎:1) to ₹50.4 lakh (30💎:1). Nobody sells a ₹16-lakh asset for ₹100.

The 625-day problem

  • To deliver one advertised credit honestly, the vendor needs 625 bot-days — 4 bots running 24/7 for 1.7 years.
  • Or: 2,500 bot-accounts playing one capped day each, which requires 2,500 guild slots that do not exist.
  • Or: they aren’t playing matches at all, in which case the glory is arriving through the server API, not through gameplay — a different crime with a much shorter detection latency.

This trilemma is the whole report in one paragraph.

Second-order reality check. If your guild is already active, the daily cap may be exhausted before the bots log in — vendors’ “0-glory auto-refund” is measured on their dashboard, so a capped guild silently converts ₹100 into zero and the refund tripwire (total zero in 2 h) is the only protection. A 45-member roster at cap earns 3,600/day; four extra bots are worth at most +8.9%, and 0% after the cap.

4. How these things are actually built

Three architectures, in descending order of engineering effort and ascending order of how often you meet them in the wild.

Input automationADB taps, scrcpy, Appium, AutoJS

Honest yield, terrible economics: 80 glory/day/bot. Detected by VM policy + AFK behaviour + device clusters.

B · Protocol client

No renderer, no gameplay
Protobuf messages
AES-CBC wrapped requests
Guest JWT auth → guild join → queue
Game TCP/HTTP endpoints
What the public repos do (FreeFireApi, OB55 TCP bot, Frida guest capture). Detected by telemetry inconsistency.

C · Theatre

YouTube → Telegram → APK
Termux Python “source”
Progress bar / fake success log
Sometimes: login prompt
Your phone or guild ID
Delivers a Telegram subscription and, on the APK branch, occasionally a credential harvester.

Architecture A is the only one that can honestly claim “real matches”. It is also the one the 2026 ban waves target by name: “accounts using hacks, modified files or virtual machines are now being permanently removed.” Architecture B skips gameplay entirely — it authenticates guest accounts, joins the guild, and posts match-result traffic. That is reverse engineering of the client protocol (ToS §4.2(j)) and, more practically, it is visible to the server as glory credited without corresponding match telemetry, which is a server-side check, not a client-side one. Architecture C is why the phrase “guild glory bot source code” has 30-second YouTube Shorts and no reproducible results.

Worth crediting the one honest design decision in this market: “Guild-ID-only, credential-free” services genuinely reduce what you can lose, because they never hold your login. It does not make the service real — it means the loss is confined to money and the guild, rather than the account.

5. Enforcement: it is no longer aimed at the bot

Garena’s Abuse Policy was updated in March 2026 with a section that exists specifically for this traffic. It is worth reading verbatim, because it changes who pays.

“Coordinated Unfair Play for Guilds. Guilds found to have engaged in organized rule-breaking — including, but not limited to, collusion, score manipulation, or the coordinated use of prohibited tools or software — will be subject to enforcement action commensurate with the severity and scale of the violation.”

The listed penalties, in escalating combination: removal of accumulated Guild Activeness and Guild War points; disqualification and ban from Guild Tournament leaderboards; permanent disbandment of the guild — “all members will lose their guild affiliation upon disbandment” — with associated accounts subject to further individual penalties.

7. The same outcome, without the exposure

The honest comparison is not “bots vs nothing”. It is “bots vs the roster you already have” — because four bots are, mechanically, four members.

The playbook that actually moves the bar

  • Never queue solo. Two-guildmate teams earn ~10 activity points/minute; each additional guildmate adds 20%.
  • Dog Tags beat everything. 1,800 tags/week unlocks the Room Card, and the four boxes pay 500 + 800 + 1,200 + 1,800 glory — about 4,300/week, free, guild-wide.
  • Daily missions are 10 points each and take minutes. Twenty members doing two = 400/day.
  • Wars: Tue / Thu / Sat. BR counts your best five matches (1st = 12 points, each elimination = 4); CS pays 100 per win. Quitting or a ban deducts.
  • Diamonds are a booster, not a strategy — ₹8.4 per glory is ~27× the honest bot rate and ~17,000× the advertised one.
  • Consistency > spikes. Settlement is Monday 04:00 with decay; a burst that decays by Friday bought nothing.

What to do instead, by goal

  • Reach Lv.2 (10,000) — 35 members × 4 days at cap, or one dog-tag cycle plus missions. ₹0.
  • Weekly Room Card — 1,800 tags ≈ 200 guild-wide matches. Recruit for daily play, not level.
  • Regional top-100 — a stable 40+ roster beats any burst; leaderboards are audited (Garena has already removed 289 accounts for rank fraud).
  • “I want it while I sleep” — nothing legitimate exists. The risk-adjusted answer is no, and the cost of the alternative is four recruitment posts.

8. If you buy anyway

Then price it as a lottery ticket, not a purchase: never share a login or install an APK (that is where the real loss lives); expect zero and treat any glory as a bonus; never run it on the same guild you would be heartbroken to lose; and assume the guild ID is public and the roster visible. The one thing not to do is the “cheap” version: a free APK or a “source code” from a Telegram channel is the only branch of this market that can cost you the account you actually play on.

9. Bottom line

Guild glory is telemetry, not currency: capped per player, capped per guild, settled weekly, and decaying. That design is precisely why a bot cannot be a shortcut — it can only be another member. Once you price four ghost members at 320 glory/day, the ₹100 credit is revealed as either a fiction or a server-side exploit, and the second option has been the target of every major ban wave of 2026. The guild-level enforcement clause means the person holding the bill is not the vendor but the leader who pasted the ID.

10. Method, confidence, and what I could not verify

How this was assembled

  • Primary: Garena support articles (Guilds FAQ, Abuse Policy, Account Ban, Anti-Hack FAQ, Game Security FAQ), Garena ToS, the Portuguese support article with the level/glory table, OB42 patch notes, and the follow-up patch note on team-up point rates.
  • Secondary: fan media (Free Fire Mania) for ban-wave figures; ScamAdviser/Gridinsoft for domain signals; GitHub repos for architecture evidence.
  • Tertiary: vendor sites and docs (treated as claims), plus SEO explainers — useful only as evidence of what the market says, never as fact.

Confidence and gaps

  • High: the caps (multiple official pages), the enforcement text (verbatim), the arithmetic.
  • Medium: current level/slot ceilings — Garena’s help pages are stale; community trackers disagree; I used ranges.
  • Medium: ban totals — relayed from regional official graphics by fan media, not audited.
  • Unverifiable: whether any specific vendor delivers anything at all. No vendor publishes match-level logs, and no independent test with a control guild exists. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling something.
